      How to play
      

        
Describe a person in six guesses or fewer. After each guess, every attribute is coloured:

        
    
          
  •  exactly right
    • 
          
  •  close; the arrow says which way to go
    • 
          
  •  wrong, and not close
    • 
          
  • ∅ your choice made no difference to the score, so it was read as the lowest option that scores the same
    • 
        

        
The choices for your next guess keep score for you: green is the one known to be right, grey ones are ruled out by a guess so far. You can still pick a grey one. Once you have something right it folds down to one line, and "change" brings its choices back. Your next guess starts from your last, minus anything it ruled out: those you choose again before you can guess.

        
You also see what your guess would score, section by section. Arrows point from your guess towards the answer: ↑ means later in the list (older, more years, a higher level). The score works the same way: each section the clue shows says whether the hidden person has more ↑ or fewer ↓ points than your guess.

      

      

        
Describe anyone at all who scores exactly the number shown, in eight guesses or fewer. There is more than one right answer and nothing is coloured.

        
After each guess you see what it scores and how far off that is. Under every choice is what one step would have changed: ▲ for the next option down the list (older, more years, a higher level), ▼ for the one before. The steps are exact for that guess, but points depend on each other, so two steps together do not always add up.

      

      

        
The clue can be the total with its four sections, the total alone, or the total with one more section shown after each miss. Each kind of clue hides a different person.

        
The four sections are the ones Canada uses: core (age, education, languages, Canadian work or a job offer), spouse, skill transferability (combinations of education, language and work), and additional points (French, a sibling, study in Canada, a provincial nomination).

        
If there is a spouse, the spouse's own points are a given and you do not have to guess them. Language levels are simplified to one level across all four abilities.

      

      

        
Five rounds. Each shows a score and four people; one of them has exactly that score. Pick who. After you pick, everyone's score is shown.

        
What a description leaves out, the person does not have: no job offer, no study in Canada, no sibling there, no nomination. Language levels are simplified to one level across all four abilities, and a spouse's own points are stated rather than explained.

      

      
Scored with the rules in force from 3 November 2026. The people are invented; nobody real is described. Not a calculator and not advice.
