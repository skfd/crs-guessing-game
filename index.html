      How to play
      
Describe a person in six guesses or fewer. After each guess, every attribute is coloured:

      
    
        
  •  exactly right
    • 
        
  •  close; the arrow says which way to go
    • 
        
  •  wrong; an arrow still shows the direction where there is one
    • 
        
  • ∅ your choice made no difference to the score, so it was read as the lowest option that scores the same
    • 
      

      
You also see what your guess would score, section by section, against the hidden person. Arrows always point from your guess towards the answer: ↑ means later in the list (older, more years, a higher level).

      
The four sections are the ones Canada uses: core (age, education, languages, Canadian work or a job offer), spouse, skill transferability (combinations of education, language and work), and additional points (French, a sibling, study in Canada, a provincial nomination).

      
If the person has a spouse, the spouse's own points are given and you do not have to guess them. Language levels are simplified to one level across all four abilities.

      
Scored with the rules in force from 3 November 2026. The people are invented; nobody real is described. Not a calculator and not advice.
